Drug Utilisation

0.1 What is a drug utilisation study?

A drug utilisation study (DUS) describes how a drug is used in clinical practice: who receives it, at what dose, for how long, for what indications, and in what combinations.

DUS are not causal analyses. They do not estimate the effect of a drug on an outcome. Their purpose is to describe, or characterise, real-world prescribing patterns. They are a key regulatory tool, regularly commissioned as post-authorisation safety studies (PASS) to monitor whether a drug is being used as intended.

Key questions a DUS answers:

  • Who is initiating the drug (demographics, comorbidities)?
  • What are the treatment patterns (dose, duration, adherence, switching)?
  • What are the indications for use?
  • Is the drug being used off-label?
  • What are co-prescriptions and concomitant medications?

0.3 Example pipeline

In this tutorial we will provide an example on how to do a DUS with DrugUtilisation, but further documentation can be found in the package documentation, with all available functions in its reference index and in this other tutorial or this training.

0.3.1 Step 1: Create a drug cohort

The DrugUtilisation package provides generateIngredientCohortSet() as its primary cohort creation function. It creates drug cohorts, collapsing consecutive prescription records with gaps below a specified threshold.

Code 
# Create semaglutide cohort as ingredient
cdm <- generateIngredientCohortSet(
  cdm        = cdm,
  name       = "semaglutide_dus_cohort",
  ingredient = "semaglutide",
  gapEra     = 365    # this number depends on how recurrent prescriptions for this ingredient's medications are
) 

cdm$semaglutide_dus_cohort <- cdm$semaglutide_dus_cohort|>
  requireIsFirstDrugEntry() |> # First entry, like before
  requireObservationBeforeDrug(days = 365) # Prior observation period, like before!

Our study: we have already done our cohorts for GLP-1 RA with the conceptCohort() function. There is no need to repeat that. These are two different options: the generateIngredientCohortSet() allows you to go for ingredient level, while the conceptCohort() allows you to use specific products and dose forms.

Quick check: What does gapEra = 365 mean in generateIngredientCohortSet()?

gapEra controls the era collapsing logic. In claims or dispensing data, a patient on a once-weekly drug might have their 4-week prescription record end on day 28 and the next refill start on day 30 — a 2-day gap. Without era collapsing, these would appear as two separate drug eras (treatment discontinuation + restart). With gapEra = 30, gaps of fewer than 30 days are bridged, producing one continuous treatment era. The appropriate gap depends on the drug’s dosing frequency and the data source’s dispensing patterns.

Our study — treatment duration metrics to report:

For each GLP-1 RA agent, report:

Metric Clinical interpretation
Days prescribed (median, IQR) The duration of the prescribed course — often longer than actual use
Days exposed (median, IQR) The duration of actual drug supply based on dispensing records
Number of drug eras Whether patients have multiple treatment episodes (treatment restarts)
Initial daily dose (mg) Whether patients are initiated at the recommended starting dose
Cumulative dose (mg) Total drug exposure over the follow-up period

0.3.3 Step 3: Indications for use

What conditions were patients treated for? This is especially relevant for GLP-1 RAs, which are licensed for T2DM and obesity but increasingly used off-label.

1 {r indication, message=FALSE, warning=FALSE} # # Define indication cohorts # indications <- list( # headache = 378253, # influenza = 4266367 # ) # # cdm <- CDMConnector::generateConceptCohortSet( # cdm = cdm, # conceptSet = indications, # name = "indications_cohort" # ) # # indication_result <- cdm$dus_cohort |> # summariseIndication( # indicationCohortName = "indications_cohort", # unknownIndicationTable = "condition_occurrence", # indicationWindow = list(c(-30, 0)) # ) # # indication_result |> # filter(estimate_name == "percentage") |> # select(variable_level, estimate_value) #

Our study — indication analysis: For GLP-1 RAs, define indication cohorts for:

  • Type 2 diabetes mellitus (primary licensed indication)
  • Obesity / overweight (licensed for semaglutide/tirzepatide in higher doses)
  • Cardiovascular disease (licensed for some agents with demonstrated CV benefit)
  • No identifiable coded indication (potential off-label use)

A high proportion of “no identifiable coded indication” may reflect coding limitations rather than true off-label use — many GPs do not re-code T2DM at every prescribing event.

1.0.1 Step 4: Treatment adherence

Proportion of Days Covered (PDC) is the standard measure of medication adherence in claims data. PDC ≥ 80% is the conventional threshold for “adherent.”

Our study — treatment switching: For GLP-1 RA DUS, switching is particularly clinically relevant: patients may switch between agents due to tolerability (nausea is common with all GLP-1 RAs), formulary changes, or lack of glycaemic response. A high rate of early switching (within 90 days) to a different GLP-1 RA would be a DUS finding worth reporting to regulators, as it might indicate a population-level tolerability pattern not captured in clinical trials.

Quick check: Your DUS shows that 35% of semaglutide users have no coded T2DM or obesity diagnosis in the 30 days before initiation. Does this mean 35% are receiving the drug off-label?

Not necessarily. In routine clinical databases, many patients with chronic conditions like T2DM do not have their diagnosis re-coded at every GP visit or prescription event. The absence of a recent coded diagnosis in the 30-day window does not mean the diagnosis is absent — it may simply not have been recorded recently. Looking at a wider window (e.g. any T2DM code ever in the patient’s history) typically reveals a much higher proportion with documented indication. The “no identifiable coded indication” category in a DUS is therefore best interpreted as a lower bound on true off-label use, not as the actual off-label rate.

3.1 Exporting the results

You can export a summarised result with all the objects that you created from your different calculations, and then read that file in a Shiny App to show the results. The package for that is OmopViewer but visOmopResults and each package own functions allow for it too.

Code 
# Suppress small cell counts before sharing
minCellCount <- 5 

exportSummarisedResult(
  du_result
  minCellCount = minCellCount,
  path = here("Results"),
  fileName = "GregonBoard_dus_{cdm_name}_{date}.csv"
)
